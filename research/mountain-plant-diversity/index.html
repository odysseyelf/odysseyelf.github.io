Origins and Assembly of Mountain Plant Diversity

Mountains support exceptionally high levels of plant diversity, yet similar patterns of species richness can arise through very different evolutionary and biogeographic processes. My research examines how geological history, colonization, ecological opportunity, and lineage diversification interact to shape the assembly of mountain floras.

By integrating phylogenomics, divergence-time estimation, historical biogeography, diversification analyses, and ecological data, I compare plant radiations across mountain systems. A central goal is to understand why some regions generate diversity primarily through rapid in situ diversification, whereas others accumulate species through repeated immigration and long-term persistence.
