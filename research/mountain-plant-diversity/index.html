Origins and Assembly of Mountain Plant Diversity

Mountains support exceptionally high levels of plant diversity, yet similar patterns of species richness can arise through very different evolutionary and biogeographic processes. My research examines how geological history, colonization, ecological opportunity, and lineage diversification interact to shape the assembly of mountain floras.

By integrating phylogenomics, divergence-time estimation, historical biogeography, diversification analyses, and ecological data, I compare plant radiations across mountain systems. A central goal is to understand why some regions generate diversity primarily through rapid in situ diversification, whereas others accumulate species through repeated immigration and long-term persistence.

Ongoing Research

Evolutionary assembly of Taiwanese mountain floras

This research investigates how repeated colonization, geographic isolation, rapid diversification, and ecological differentiation contributed to the exceptional endemic plant diversity of Taiwan.

Comparative evolution of mountain plant radiations

I compare lineages distributed across major mountain systems to determine how geological timing, ecological opportunity, and lineage-specific traits influence diversification and regional species accumulation.

Macroevolution of Androsace

This project reconstructs the phylogenetic, biogeographic, and diversification history of Androsace and related genera, with particular emphasis on the European Alps and Asian mountains.

Ecological differentiation across elevational gradients

I examine how climatic niches and elevational distributions affect lineage persistence, geographic range evolution, and the assembly of plant diversity in heterogeneous mountain environments.

Published Research

Accepted

Global biogeography and diversification of Berberis

Nature Communications

This study compares the evolutionary assembly of species-rich Berberis radiations in the Hengduan–Himalayan Mountains and the Andes, showing that comparable mountain diversity can emerge through contrasting combinations of colonization, diversification, and functional traits.
