# Research My research aims to understand how plant diversity originates and accumulates through time and space by integrating **phylogenomics**, **systematics**, **historical biogeography**, and **macroevolutionary analyses**. Using mountain systems and continental islands as natural laboratories, I investigate how geological history, ecological opportunity, and evolutionary processes interact to generate exceptional plant diversity and endemism.
## Origins and Assembly of Mountain Plant Diversity Mountain regions harbor extraordinary levels of plant diversity despite sharing many similar environmental conditions. I investigate how colonization history, geological processes, ecological opportunity, and evolutionary innovations collectively shape the assembly of mountain floras across the world. Learn more →
## Historical Biogeography and Macroevolution By integrating dated phylogenies with probabilistic biogeographic models, I reconstruct the geographic and evolutionary histories of plant lineages to understand how dispersal, extinction, diversification, and trait evolution have shaped global biodiversity patterns. Learn more →
## Systematics and Phylogenomics I use genome-scale data to resolve evolutionary relationships, delimit species, and reconstruct phylogenetic histories. My research combines phylogenomics, comparative genomics, and taxonomy to establish robust evolutionary frameworks for studying biodiversity. Learn more →
## Plant Endemism on Continental Islands Continental islands provide exceptional opportunities to investigate how repeated colonization, geographic isolation, ecological differentiation, and in situ diversification contribute to the origin and maintenance of endemic plant diversity. Taiwan serves as one of my principal study systems for addressing these questions. Learn more →
