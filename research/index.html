Research

My research explores how evolutionary history, environmental change, ecological opportunity, and functional traits interact to shape the origin and assembly of plant diversity, particularly in mountain systems and continental islands.

Mountain Plant Diversity

Why do some mountain regions become exceptional centers of plant diversity? I investigate how colonization, ecological opportunity, functional innovation, and geological history jointly influence diversification in mountain floras.

Learn more →

Historical Biogeography

I reconstruct how dispersal, extinction, range evolution, and diversification have shaped the geographic histories of plant lineages across continents and through geological time.

Learn more →

Plant Functional Ecology

I examine how functional traits, physiological tolerances, and ecological niches influence adaptation, range limits, lineage persistence, and diversification across environmental gradients.

Learn more →

Island Evolution and Endemism

Using continental islands such as Taiwan, I study how repeated colonization, ecological differentiation, geographic isolation, and in situ diversification contribute to endemic plant diversity.
