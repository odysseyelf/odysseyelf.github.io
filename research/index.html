Research

My research aims to understand how plant diversity originates and accumulates through time and space by integrating phylogenomics, systematics, historical biogeography, and macroevolutionary analyses. Using mountain systems and continental islands as natural laboratories, I investigate how geological history, ecological opportunity, and evolutionary processes interact to generate exceptional plant diversity and endemism.

Origins and Assembly of Mountain Plant Diversity

Mountain regions harbor extraordinary levels of plant diversity. I investigate how colonization history, geological processes, ecological opportunity, and evolutionary innovations collectively shape the assembly and diversification of mountain floras.

Historical Biogeography and Macroevolution

By integrating dated phylogenies with probabilistic biogeographic models, I reconstruct how dispersal, extinction, diversification, and trait evolution have shaped the geographic histories and global diversity patterns of plant lineages.

Systematics and Phylogenomics

I use genome-scale data to resolve evolutionary relationships, evaluate species boundaries, and reconstruct phylogenetic histories. These systematic frameworks provide the foundation for studying biogeography, diversification, and trait evolution.

Plant Endemism on Continental Islands

Continental islands provide natural laboratories for examining how colonization, geographic isolation, ecological differentiation, and in situ diversification generate endemic plant diversity. Taiwan is a principal study system for addressing these questions.
