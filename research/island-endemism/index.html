Plant Endemism on Continental Islands

Oceanic islands have long served as model systems for studying evolution, yet many continental islands also harbor exceptionally high levels of endemic biodiversity despite their repeated connections to adjacent continents. My research asks how endemic species arise on geologically young continental islands and whether the evolutionary processes generating their biodiversity resemble those of classic oceanic island radiations.

By integrating phylogenomics, population-level sampling, historical biogeography, ecological niche modeling, and comparative analyses, I investigate how geological history, dispersal, ecological opportunity, and geographic isolation interact to promote diversification in island plant lineages.

Ongoing Research

How does endemic diversity arise on a geologically young continental island?

Taiwan is a geologically young continental island with an exceptionally high level of plant endemism. This project investigates the evolutionary origin of Taiwan's endemic Berberis species using phylogenomics, historical biogeography, and ecological niche modeling.

Contrary to earlier morphology-based hypotheses proposing multiple independent colonizations, our analyses indicate that most endemic Taiwanese Berberis species originated through a single in situ radiation after colonization of the island. However, one newly discovered species, Berberis dandaensis, represents an independent evolutionary lineage, revealing that Taiwan's endemic flora has multiple historical components.

Ecological niche modeling further suggests that although the Taiwan Strait was exposed during glacial periods, the resulting land bridge provided little suitable habitat for these cold-adapted mountain shrubs. Instead, colonization likely occurred through a series of coastal stepping-stone habitats along southeastern China before establishment in Taiwan.

Following colonization, rapid mountain uplift, strong elevational gradients, and marked differences in moisture availability promoted repeated shifts into high-elevation environments and subsequent adaptive diversification. These results suggest that despite its continental origin, Taiwan can generate evolutionary dynamics remarkably similar to those observed on classic oceanic islands, where restricted gene flow and ecological opportunity drive endemic radiations.

Future Directions

Future research will combine denser population sampling with high-resolution genomic approaches to investigate the ecological and historical mechanisms underlying speciation in Taiwan's endemic Berberis. I also plan to expand this research framework to other rapidly diversified woody plant groups on continental islands, including Cotoneaster, to identify general mechanisms driving island endemism.
